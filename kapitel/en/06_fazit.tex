\chapter{Conclusion and Outlook}\label{ch:conclusion}

This chapter takes stock: it answers the research questions RQ0--RQ4 from Section~\ref{sec:rqs}
together with their sub-questions, names the limits of the state reached, and sketches the next
steps.

\section{Answering the Research Questions}\label{sec:answers}
Orthogonal to all five questions, the introduction had deliberately left a categorization question
open: whether the axes of the design space separate cleanly into organ, system, and measurement
axes --- and how such a separation is to be accomplished where it does not yet exist. Both are
answered (Section~\ref{sec:axis-triad}): the axes separate along the three concerns into three
\emph{realms} with one class root each, one offer catalog each, and one stamp line each at the
binary (Section~\ref{sec:stamp-model}). The separation was accomplished not by renaming but by
datable \emph{relocations}: telemetry and the ISA left the organ count for the system sphere,
compiler and scheduling moved from the former five-item system list into the complex bracket and
onto the ISA wrapper respectively, and the load framework left the system world without replacement
towards the measurement realm; in the code, compile-time departure guards fix these relocations
(Section~\ref{sec:impl-system-axes}). This triad is the second shape of the separability problem
and carries the answers to the following sub-questions.

\paragraph{RQ0 (Main question).} By how much active, measurement-driven cache-aware decisions
outperform their passive, statically dimensioned counterparts can be quantified conclusively only
after the end-to-end runs per platform (Section~\ref{sec:outlook}). What is prepared is the
apparatus that isolates exactly this difference: the same axis-constant structure is permuted once
actively (measurement-driven page-type, layout, prefetch, and value-placement decision) and once
passively (statically dimensioned) and compared under an identical compiler and flag basis on
hybrid CPUs and Sapphire Rapids. The extension sub-question --- whether a measured gain can be
attributed unambiguously at all --- is answered structurally, and the separation it requires is
precisely the triad: an organ effect permutes the \texttt{binary\_id}, a platform concern stands at
the binary as system stamp and build version identifier without permuting it, and a measurement
concern remains strictly binary-id-neutral --- no measurement instrument may change \emph{what} is
built.

\paragraph{RQ1 (Composition).} The canonical decomposition carries: \emph{eighteen} organ main
axes in fixed slot order T0--T17 span the permutation matrix (Section~\ref{sec:axis-triad}); the
former nineteen-axis count (interim state of July 2026) is a historical precursor of this order.
The canonical measurement catalog --- two enabled representatives per organ axis, the persistence
axis pinned to its in-memory representative --- counts $2^{17} = 131072$ binary identities per
system permutation, each with a full eighteen-segment path; the raw variant product space across
all axis catalogs including their sub-axes lies orders of magnitude above that
(Section~\ref{sec:mess-chain}). State-of-the-art designs are reconstructed unambiguously through
algorithm-profile XMLs: 30 SOTA profiles (8 Rank-1 + 22 Rank-2/3) demonstrate that the axis
decomposition carries P01--P33, and the variadic API specialization (1/2/$N$ parameters onto
\texttt{std::vector}/\texttt{std::map}/tuple) makes both algorithm- and container-oriented
patterns expressible on one engine. The sub-question on the category cut is answered in the
affirmative: the decomposition remains orthogonal only because platform and measurement concerns
are kept out of the permutation matrix --- the cut was accomplished by the dated relocations and
is held by the departure guards in the code (Section~\ref{sec:impl-system-axes}). The
non-fully-orthogonal organ couplings (node type $\times$ SIMD $\times$ path compression) are
marked as such and explicitly restricted during permutation. The sub-question on cache-update and
cache-line-fill behaviour under a solely varied ISA/SIMD axis is answered methodically ---
precisely \emph{because} the SIMD extension is not an organ axis: as a build control quantity it
multiplies only the build matrix, so that one and the same \texttt{binary\_id} can be built under
several system permutations and compared with an otherwise unchanged structure
(Section~\ref{sec:axis-triad}); the empirical answer follows with the full-build runs
(Section~\ref{sec:outlook}).

\paragraph{RQ2 (Measurement methodology).} Measurement is split into a production mode
(\texttt{COMDARE\_\allowbreak{}MEASUREMENT\_\allowbreak{}ON} disabled, no overhead) and an
experiment mode: the \texttt{ResultAggregator} is kept host-side in the \texttt{ExperimentDriver}
(phases~5--7), and the experiment mode activates the measurement hooks and the observer coupling
in the ABI adapter. Profile-aware workload routing selects the workload per permutation from the
traversal tag, ensuring fair comparison between range-query- and point-lookup-oriented designs;
this routing concerns only the default assignment of series~A, while the answer to the home-field
bias remains the bias-free full matrix, which in whole-system benchmarking runs all load profiles
as a dynamic axis against all living-being binaries (Chapter~\ref{ch:measurement}). Each series is
collected at three granularities: micro-benchmarking measures each axis over its \emph{axis
interface}, macro- and whole-system benchmarking add the genus interface operations and the
load-profile level, and three wall-clock levels check the internal micro-measurement from the
outside (Section~\ref{sec:mess-chain}). The sub-question on the measurement axes is answered in
the affirmative: the measurement instruments are a realm of their own in the planner --- the load
framework as its first main axis, the measurement tooling with the sixteen categories as a
sub-axis ---, always present as soon as measurement is on, and strictly binary-id-neutral; this
separation was accomplished by the double root cut --- the system realm is rooted at the builder,
the measurement realm at the planner (Section~\ref{sec:axis-triad}). Contradictory findings in the
literature on the optimal node/cache-line size (one versus up to sixteen cache lines) are thus not
adopted from incompatible original setups but re-measured axis-isolated, with all else held equal.
The extensibility sub-question is answered in concept and honestly unfinished in build-out: a new
probe joins as a living being of its genus --- it docks at the probe dock, passes the three-stage
check, and its measurement schema (replace, hybrid, or full-join mode) remains distinguishable at
the binary via the \texttt{e} suffix of the version tag and the hierarchically extended algorithm
names of the organ line --- no stamp line of its own is needed for that, because the probe mixes
against the organ axes (Section~\ref{sec:eval-results}); whether it matches an
existing category, founds a new one, or constitutes a hybrid form is decided by the genus cut --- a genus
of its own would arise only with a fundamentally different base axis set
(Section~\ref{sec:anatomy-levels}). The operating mode asked for there, which offers the design
components of exactly \emph{one} publication as a closed axis set in isolation, is by contrast not
built and remains outlook (Section~\ref{sec:outlook}). A platform counts as successfully verified
only once the conformance gate confirms the \texttt{std::map} contract per module and the platform
probe covers the reported axis subset; the platform-calibrated thresholds themselves are fixed
with the real runs.

\paragraph{RQ3 (Probe comparison).} The concrete comparison of PRT-ART against the 30 SOTA
profiles is pending the first measurement runs (Section~\ref{sec:outlook}). The architecture is
fully prepared: the PRT-ART profile, the three mandatory series templates, the
\texttt{ExperimentDriver} with auto-pickup of the SOTA profiles, and the ABI-conformant adapter
with the \texttt{std::map} core contract (conformance subset, Appendix~\ref{app:interfaces}); the
full expansion to all seventeen contract operations including the notify hooks is designated as a
follow-up step. The cheapest local page representation per branch point (point versus compact
page) and its platform-calibrated switching rules are likewise part of the PRT-ART profile. The
compile-time sub-question --- whether latency-critical measurement forces decisions selectable at
run time as such into compile time --- is answered in the affirmative and has become the
construction principle: every axis divides into a compile-time-fixed \emph{main} decision and
runtime-variable \emph{sub}-decisions; the main axes are compiled in statically --- for organ axes
as a \texttt{binary\_id} segment, for system axes as stamp plus build version identifier ---,
while dynamic sub-axes vary their runtime parameters without recompilation
(Section~\ref{sec:measurement-system}). Exactly the division the sub-question conjectured is thus
the carrying doctrine of the apparatus; RQ4 continues it.

\paragraph{RQ4 (Compile time vs.\ run time).} The boundary between compile time and run time runs
along the axis categories, and the system extension sub-question is thereby answered in the
affirmative: the platform concerns --- the three system main axes together with their complex
bracket (Section~\ref{sec:complex-axis}) --- belong to the compile-time identity of the binary;
they appear as system stamp line and build version identifier, never as a \texttt{binary\_id}
segment, and produce provenance-verifiable binaries per permutation (SHA256-validated original
bodies --- whose full implementation Section~\ref{sec:limitations} lists as open ---,
\texttt{get\_compiler()} identifier). Measurement choices remain runtime-variable, and layout and
page selection remain selectable at run time via a bit-encoded permutation identifier, without
violating the common compiler and flag basis. Where an axis needs both sides of the boundary, it
is \emph{two-way-split}: load framework, telemetry, and hardware detection run across the runtime
of the planner \emph{and} the compile time of the builder --- the sub-axis release of the earlier
stage becomes the main-axis assumption of the later one (Section~\ref{sec:axis-triad}) ---, and
the two-way-split hardware detection uses its level~2 to set the compile-time stamps of the tier
binaries correctly from the actual properties acquired at builder runtime
(Section~\ref{sec:hw-probe}). What is stamped is always the compile-time platform \emph{class},
never a measured value; as a \emph{design}, the concept of a \emph{fit stamp} follows on from
this: it would check the stamped platform class on every measurement run against the named runtime
verdict of the hardware detection (Section~\ref{sec:impl-system-axes}) --- the fit between machine
and binary would thereby itself become a checked quantity; in the state of this work this concept
is design, not built (Section~\ref{sec:outlook}). The forward-looking sub-question of whether the
measurement itself can tailor ISA- and operating-system-optimized binaries for a recombination is
taken up by the outlook as heuristics automation (Section~\ref{sec:outlook}); the
operating-system dimension is already anchored in the measurement plan via the two measurement
regimes from Chapter~\ref{ch:eval}.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Module bodies:} the ad-hoc codegen of the active measurement path emits real
  axis-library bodies per permutation (in the state of this work the eighteen fully qualified axis
  types plus the umbrella include); only the legacy profile codegen still produces ABI-conformant
  skeleton modules, and the remaining gaps are the PRT-ART probe stub and the honestly
  zero-reported storage/prefetch axes of the pool families.
  \item \textbf{Full build and delivery measurement pending:} the declared build target
  (Section~\ref{sec:eval-method}) --- per system permutation the $2^{17}$ organ permutations of
  the canonical catalog --- has not yet been run, nor has the delivery measurement over the small,
  materializable 320-identity catalog (Section~\ref{sec:impl-contracts}); the end-to-end
  \texttt{messung\_driver} runs across the target platforms (AVX2/AVX-512, ARM NEON/SVE) are
  outstanding, while the apparatus-side acceptance per package already runs through the two-gate
  protocol.
  \item \textbf{Results chapter:} currently methodological; concrete measurements follow. A first
  real smoke measurement series (43 permutations) is available in Appendix~\ref{app:measurements},
  but does not replace the mandatory measurement series.
  \item \textbf{Hardware counters off by default and partly source-dependent:} PMC capture (L1, L3
  and dTLB misses via \texttt{perf\_event\_open}) is disabled by default; L2 misses and coherence
  invalidations stay structurally at zero regardless of the switch for lack of a portable counter,
  the last-level counter can additionally fail to open on individual AMD platforms, and energy
  capture via RAPL is wired up best-effort but stays empty without root or zone read
  access\@; branch misses and IPC are planned as an extension of the
  counter set and are currently not captured. In the smoke series so far
  the hardware columns are zero --- the cache-aware node/data/index measurement component is thus
  still pending (Figure~\ref{fig:measurement-triangle}).
  \item \textbf{Hardware detection in partial build-out:} the first two build-out stages ---
  provenance vocabulary, SPD parser, and error domain as well as acquisition chain, compile-time
  factory, and runtime verdict --- are available as code (Section~\ref{sec:impl-system-axes});
  outstanding are the wiring into runner and probe dock, the additive result write-back including
  its consumers, the provenance round trip of the machine declarations, and the fit-stamp check
  (Sections~\ref{sec:hw-probe} and~\ref{sec:answers}).
  \item \textbf{Evaluation encoding:} CSV is the implemented state; the xlsx factory writer
  decided as the future default (one sheet per selected sub-axis permutation plus an info sheet,
  Section~\ref{sec:eval-results}) is not yet built.
  \item \textbf{Smoke series with apparatus caveats:} the real measurements so far vary seven axes
  of the then nineteen-axis count (interim state of July 2026) in two sub-blocks (27
  SIMD$\times$layout$\times$allocator combinations plus 16 combinations over node width, path
  compression, internal search, and telemetry), and partly approximate axis effects via wall-clock
  proxies; single effects are apparatus rather than organ artefacts (\enquote{shadowing}). The
  complete limitation table is given in Section~\ref{sec:measurements:limitations}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Outlook}\label{sec:outlook}
In the short term: the full build following the declared target (Section~\ref{sec:eval-method}),
the delivery measurement over the 320-identity catalog, the end-to-end run of the three mandatory
series on real hardware including the generation of the measurement diagrams, the build-out of the
hardware detection by wiring, result write-back, and fit stamp, the decided xlsx factory writer,
and the closing of the remaining module-body gaps (PRT-ART stub, honest-0 storage/prefetch axes of
the pool families). To this is added the paper-isolation mode designated as outlook in RQ2: an
operating mode that offers the design components of exactly one publication as a closed axis set
in isolation, in order to measure them faithfully to the original --- with measurement and system
axes remaining free-standing.

\paragraph{Heuristic automation and binary delivery.} The mechanism demanded by the thesis assignment
(sub-task~7), which automatically derives the best configuration from the measurement results, is
deliberately not yet implemented in its heuristics-driven \emph{full} automation and is reserved for a
follow-up phase; a first increment of the automatic selection and binary shipping, however, is
already available as a standalone tool (\texttt{best\_binary\_selector}) that ranks and delivers the
best permutation per metric from the measurement CSVs. The full expansion presupposes three
components; the second is already implemented, and toward the first and the third this tool
provides a first, purely measurement-driven increment: (i) a \emph{heuristic/ML classifier} that maps the load-profile and axis
features (operation mix, key characteristics, hardware identifier) to the recommended axis configuration;
(ii) the already implemented \emph{XML parser} (import and export including the extractor) for the heuristic policies stored as an XML load-profile result and reusable per
architecture focus; and (iii) a \emph{binary packager} that delivers the thus-selected overall algorithm
as a standalone binary behind the unified search-algorithm interface. A prerequisite for all three is that
the end-to-end measurement series be fully available beforehand --- only the robust data basis across all
permutations and load profiles makes a measurement-driven, automatically controlled selection meaningful;
until then, the configuration recommendations of the evaluation scheme from
Section~\ref{sec:eval-results} will initially be derived manually once the data are available.

\medskip
In the medium to long term: a GPU adapter (Grace Hopper cluster, ZIH), PIM-allocator integration (A16),
and formal verification of selected permutation classes (StarMalloc-style, A13). The three-repository
architecture lets future students add further probe algorithms analogously to PRT-ART without modifying the
cache-engine building-block library --- a key design principle for the scalability of the research infrastructure.
Beyond the tree-like search structures treated here, the anatomy principle extends through higher-level
abstractions to the further structure classes of Section~\ref{sec:overview} --- hash-based, spatial,
and flat structures ---, so that the same measurement and permutation apparatus
can be applied to entirely new classes without rebuilding it; herein lies a key lever for future
experimental progress.
